**Headline outcome:**

Most dramatic: a **compounding-convention mismatch** that inflated headline total return from a coherent **628.6%** to a fictional **2,272,346%** (four orders of magnitude). Sharpe and bucket probabilities were unaffected — scale-invariant diagnostics didn't catch it.

The pattern is reusable for Phase 5 cohort backtests — TODO to extract to a shared module.
- Realistic execution kills the canonical FTC(0.60, 0.90) edge. Taker scenario: −1.42¢/share (−2.4% ROI). Passive: +2.98¢/share (+5.0%). **But three of four legs are fallback-dominated** — the diagnostic flags this; the "edge" numbers are mostly the assumed `fallback_cents=3¢` with extra labelling.
- "Data-supported subset" filter (drop fallback-dominated rows) makes edges *worse*, not cleaner. The markets that print follow-up fills are markets where price is drifting (which is why a follow-up exists). Observed entry slip on subset: 5-7¢ vs 3¢ assumed elsewhere. Selection bias toward adverse selection.
- Weather markets are structurally thin: **43% of crosses have NO follow-up fill within 30 minutes**. Wider windows include drift, not execution cost. No fill model closes the gap.

**Verdict:** shelve. Revisit only if a structurally better fill model (CLOB-WebSocket-derived bid/ask quotes, not trade-print proxies) becomes available.

### 🔁 same-day revision — WS-passive execution flips the verdict

Within the same session, the analysis pivoted from taker → passive execution model and the deployability verdict revised. Worth highlighting because the lesson generalises hard.

- **The taker verdict ("shelve") was an artefact of the assumed execution model.** Next-fill price proxy measures what *other takers* paid; under WS-passive limit-posting via CLOB WebSocket, it doesn't apply at all. The right metric becomes fill-rate × exact-price math.
- **WS-passive verdict**: `(p_in=0.50, p_out=0.90)` is deployable. ~2,283 fills/yr (≈6/day) at +6% ROI per filled trade under optimistic-exit assumption. Lower `p_in` is the right choice — more cross events × more headroom to TP.
- **Real performance is bounded by queue priority on the exit ask.** Canonical (0.60, 0.90) shows +0.87% (optimistic) to −2.5% (strict) ROI — that's the deployable-vs-not range, determined by your actual fill mix on the exit side. There's no offline way to close this gap; only small-live measurement does.
- **Next concrete action**: 2-4 weeks small-live at (0.50, 0.90) under WS-passive. Focus cities Seoul/Shanghai/Tokyo/Wellington/London. Capital small enough to absorb 2.5% drawdown as a learning cost. Log fill mix vs the data's `exit_opp_dir` distribution, reconcile against the optimistic-strict bracket.

**Two new methodology lessons captured** in `methodology-lessons.md` (now 10 entries on top from today):
9. Execution-model assumption is a first-class research input — not an afterthought to the slippage proxy. Same data, different model, opposite verdict.
10. Queue priority is the dominant deployability question for passive strategies. The optimistic-strict bracket = the deployable range. Only small-live measurement bounds it.

**Scope corrections to earlier lessons** (re-read with execution-model framing):
- "Constant slippage with labelling" applies to *taker* trade-print proxies. Not to quote-based or fill-rate-based metrics.
- "Data-supported subsets are more expensive" is a *taker* finding. Under passive execution, the "data-supported subset" is the trading set by definition.
